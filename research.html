Research

Medical data is often far from perfect. Labels are few because experts are busy, and information is spread across images, health records and text. My work asks one question: how can a model learn well from the data we actually have?

AI VIET NAM · 12/2025 – now

Segmentation with few labels

Most histopathology images have no pixel-level labels. In CoDiR, the model's confidence guides a diffusion model that refines the predicted masks. In UniSemAlign, image features are aligned with text prototypes of each class. Both learn from a small labeled set and many unlabeled images.

Segmentation with weak labels

Weak labels are cheaper than pixel masks, but they hold less information. I reproduced CAM-based methods and ran experiments on the BCSS-WSSS and LUAD-HistoSeg datasets for ProBAG.

Bachelor thesis · 2024 – 2025

Graphs for health records

Health records hold many linked facts about each patient. In my thesis, I used graph neural networks on electronic health records to predict diabetes. This work led to two papers at Vietnamese national conferences (FAIR 2024 and VNICT 2024).

Medical data in industry

Drug data comes from many places and in many formats. I built pipelines to collect it, normalized drug names with rules and embeddings, and built a RAG system that answers questions from drug package inserts.
